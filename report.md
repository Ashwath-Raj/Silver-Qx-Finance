## Routing and placement experiment

The same p=1 logical QAOA circuit and angles were transpiled using three strategies (trivial/basic, trivial/SABRE, SABRE/SABRE) on both processor topologies, with five compiler seeds per combination. The simulation was ideal Aer with 4,096 shots per trial.

| Processor | Strategy | Median CX | Median depth | Median SWAP diagnostic | Mean valid probability | Mean optimal probability |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | trivial/basic | 27 | 53 | 5 | 0.5621 | 0.1087 |
| A | trivial/SABRE | 18 | 34 | 2 | 0.5577 | 0.1089 |
| A | SABRE/SABRE | 18 | 44 | 2 | 0.5620 | 0.1082 |
| B | trivial/basic | 36 | 56 | 8 | 0.5570 | 0.1090 |
| B | trivial/SABRE | 24 | 39 | 4 | 0.5585 | 0.1051 |
| B | SABRE/SABRE | 15 | 33 | 1 | 0.5607 | 0.1098 |

The observed resource reductions were strongest on Processor B with SABRE layout plus SABRE routing. On Processor A, trivial layout plus SABRE routing had the lowest observed median depth. Probability differences are small and should not be treated as significant from five seeds. SWAP counts come from a separate diagnostic compile that keeps SWAP operations visible, while CX/depth are from native-basis compiled circuits.
